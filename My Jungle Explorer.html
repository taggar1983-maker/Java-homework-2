What is an Jungle

Jungles are dense tropical forests found near the equator, home to over half of Earth’s species and known for their thick vegetation, high humidity, and extraordinary biodiversity. Jungles are thick, tangled forests full of amazing plants and animals, usually found in warm, rainy places near the equator.but What is a Jungle? A jungle is a type of forest with dense plants, vines, and trees that make it hard to walk through. Unlike rainforests, jungles let more sunlight reach the ground, which helps many plants grow on the forest floor. Jungles are usually found near the equator in tropical climates, where it is hot and humid, and it rains a lot.

Jungle facts

Jungles are overgrown areas with dense vegetation, allowing more sunlight to reach the forest floor than rainforests. They grow in warm, humid climates with heavy rainfall, typically near the equator. Biodiversity and Ecosystems Jungles contain more than 50% of the world’s species, making them one of the most biodiverse habitats on Earth. They include unique animals such as capybaras, poison dart frogs, and countless insects and plants. Many jungle plants are used in modern medicines, and jungles are vital for global food security.
